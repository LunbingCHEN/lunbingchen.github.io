Flight intelligence in complex environmental flows.

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the J. C. Wu Center for Aerodynamics at Shanghai Jiao Tong University. My research spans bio-inspired flight, dynamic soaring, aerodynamics, and autonomous flight, with a focus on low-energy, long-endurance UAV flight in complex environmental flows. My first-author work has been published in iScience [1] and the Journal of Fluid Mechanics [2], with another study accepted for publication in Cell Reports Physical Science [3] and a manuscript under external review at Nature Communications [4].

My doctoral research connects two complementary pathways: reducing energy expenditure through passive aerodynamic disturbance mitigation and harvesting atmospheric energy through closed-loop dynamic soaring. Across these problems, I combine unsteady aerodynamics, animal flight, reinforcement learning, and autonomous aircraft systems to connect physical mechanisms with flight implementation.

Selected Research

Dynamic soaring

Energy harvest versus directional flight

3-DOF flight dynamics · direct collocation · IPOPT · flight-envelope analysis

I formulated a 3-DOF glider model and a direct-collocation optimal-control framework to treat dynamic soaring as both energy harvesting and directed navigation. The resulting flight envelopes quantify how harvested atmospheric energy must be allocated between sustained soaring and progress toward a destination.

  • Mapped the Pareto trade-off between energy extraction and directional speed across wind conditions.
  • Connected optimized trajectories with the step-selection behavior observed in soaring birds.
Paper · iScience Full figure
Feedback intelligence

Learning step-level dynamic soaring

Soft Actor–Critic · curriculum learning · observation/reward ablation · local sensing

I built a continuous-control reinforcement-learning environment to test whether dynamic soaring requires explicit cycle-level planning. Maximum-entropy Soft Actor–Critic policies show that robust omnidirectional navigation can emerge from step-level state feedback using only local observations.

  • Used curriculum learning and systematic observation/reward ablations to identify the sensing and feedback variables required for flight.
  • Recovered an interpretable policy coordinating turning, vertical motion, and kinetic-energy management across wind conditions.
Preprint · arXiv Full figure
Flight implementation

Closed-loop dynamic soaring in the field

PX4 · Gazebo · Raspberry Pi 5 · state estimation · flight control

I am developing a fixed-wing platform that turns dynamic soaring from an optimized trajectory into a closed-loop autonomous flight system. The modular architecture integrates onboard state and wind sensing, feedback decision-making, safety supervision, and real-time control execution.

  • Built a Gazebo/PX4 simulation and embedded-integration workflow around a Raspberry Pi 5.
  • Conducted field flight tests and manual fixed-wing piloting for platform validation and controller development.
Robust flight

Passive aerodynamic robustness

Wind-tunnel testing · grid-generated turbulence · SPIV · aerodynamic sensitivity

Before active control responds, wing morphology can limit how strongly environmental disturbances are amplified. I compared avian and planform-matched engineered wings using precision force measurements, hot-wire anemometry, multi-section SPIV, and flight-dynamic analysis.

  • Quantified lower aerodynamic sensitivity, smoother stall, and a broader passive stability envelope for the avian wing.
  • Connected wing-level disturbance response to system-level stability and control demand.
Accepted · Cell Reports Physical Science Full figure
Experimental platform

Passive-first flapping mechanics

Robotic model · compliant mechanism · active–passive comparison · wind-tunnel testing

I develop robotic model systems that separate passive structural response from prescribed actuation. The platform switches between motor-driven and spring-driven flapping, enabling controlled studies of how compliance reshapes loads and motion.

  • Designed a reconfigurable experimental mechanism for active–passive comparison.
  • Connected biological motion principles with repeatable engineering experiments.
Load regulation

Load shifting in reconfigurable structures

Flow–structure interaction · time-resolved PIV · force decomposition · low-order modeling

I used programmable towing-tank experiments, precision force measurements, high-speed imaging, and time-resolved multi-plane PIV to determine how passive reconfiguration redistributes transient aerodynamic loads.

  • Identified a stiffness regime that shifts load from the acceleration peak into the post-peak trough without sacrificing mean force.
  • Linked load shifting to radial vorticity redistribution, non-axisymmetric vortex rings, and vortex breakdown.
Paper · JFM Full figure
Biological flight

How geese achieve stationary takeoff

Live-animal flight · wing kinematics · laser-sheet visualization · high-speed imaging

Analysis of 578 stationary takeoffs shows that complex goose-wing motion collapses onto a low-dimensional structure. Accelerated downstroke, wake capture, and rapid tip reversal work together to produce the vertical and horizontal forces required for takeoff.

  • Combined live-bird kinematics, force reconstruction, mass modeling, and evolving-wake visualization.
  • Translated biological wing strategies into mechanisms relevant to agile aircraft.
Preprint · arXiv Full figure
