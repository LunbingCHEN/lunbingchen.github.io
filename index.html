Environmental-energy flight

Closed-loop dynamic soaring for autonomous aircraft.

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the J. C. Wu Center for Aerodynamics, Shanghai Jiao Tong University. My research focuses on bio-inspired flight in complex environmental flows and closed-loop dynamic soaring. I combine unsteady aerodynamics, animal flight, reinforcement learning, and autonomous aircraft systems to understand how flyers exploit environmental flows for robust and energy-efficient flight.

My current thesis develops dynamic soaring as a feedback-driven energy-harvesting navigation problem, connecting passive flow mechanisms, biological flight strategies, step-level feedback decision-making, and closed-loop flight implementation.

Selected research

From flow physics to autonomous flight

My research is organized around one question: how can an aircraft use local information to harvest environmental energy while still making reliable progress toward a destination? The projects below move from the core dynamic-soaring problem to feedback intelligence, physical robustness, and experimental realization.

01 · UnderstandReveal how environmental flows provide energy and impose disturbances.
02 · DecideExtract local feedback rules for energy-aware, target-directed flight.
03 · FlyIntegrate sensing, control, and safety on a fixed-wing aircraft.
Dynamic soaring

Energy harvest versus directional flight

Flight mechanics · trajectory optimization · biological validation

Dynamic soaring is not only an energy-harvesting maneuver. A flyer must also move toward a destination, and the trajectories that maximize energy gain conflict with those that maximize directional progress.

  • Introduced directional flight as a co-equal objective of dynamic soaring.
  • Identified a step-selection strategy that alternates energy-harvest and directional-flight priorities.

Feedback intelligence

Learning step-level dynamic soaring

Local sensing · state feedback · deep reinforcement learning

I use reinforcement learning as a scientific tool to ask whether dynamic soaring requires explicit cycle-level planning. The learned policies show that robust omnidirectional navigation can emerge from step-level feedback using only local observations.

  • Recovered an interpretable control structure coordinating turning, vertical motion, and kinetic-energy management.
  • Demonstrated generalization across target directions, wind speeds, and shear-layer thicknesses.
Preprint · arXiv Full figure
Robust flight

Passive aerodynamic robustness

Avian wings · turbulence · stability · control demand

Before active control responds, the wing itself can limit how strongly flow disturbances are amplified. Compared with a planform-matched engineered wing, the avian wing shows lower aerodynamic sensitivity, smoother stall, and a broader passive stability envelope.

  • Connected wing-level response gain to system-level flight stability.
  • Revealed an efficiency–robustness trade-off relevant to bird-scale aircraft design.
Preprint · arXiv Full figure
Experimental platform

Passive-first flapping mechanics

Robotic model · compliant mechanism · wind-tunnel testing

I develop robotic model systems that separate passive structural response from prescribed actuation. The platform switches between motor-driven and spring-driven flapping, enabling controlled studies of how compliance reshapes loads and motion.

  • Designed a reconfigurable experimental mechanism for active–passive comparison.
  • Connected biological motion principles with repeatable engineering experiments.
Load regulation

Load shifting in reconfigurable structures

Flow–structure interaction · PIV · vortex dynamics · low-order modeling

Flexible plates do not simply reduce force. Within a specific stiffness regime, they shift load from the acceleration peak into the post-peak trough, reducing fluctuations without sacrificing mean force.

  • Combined high-resolution force measurement with time-resolved PIV.
  • Linked load shifting to the radial vorticity distribution, non-axisymmetric vortex rings, and vortex breakdown.
Paper · JFM Full figure
Biological flight

How geese achieve stationary takeoff

Animal flight · wing kinematics · wake capture · flow visualization

Analysis of 578 stationary takeoffs shows that complex goose-wing motion collapses onto a low-dimensional structure. Accelerated downstroke, wake capture, and rapid tip reversal work together to produce the vertical and horizontal forces required for takeoff.

  • Combined live-bird kinematics, force reconstruction, mass modeling, and flow visualization.
  • Translated biological wing strategies into mechanisms relevant to agile aircraft.
